Red flags present

  • Payment demanded within 48 hours, by gift card or wire
  • No itemised amount — a single round total
  • Return address is a mailbox service, not an office
  • Threatens arrest, which a collector cannot do

What a legitimate one must contain

  • The creditor's name and the amount owed, itemised
  • Your validation rights and the 30-day window to dispute
  • A statement identifying it as an attempt to collect a debt
  • A working address and telephone number for the collector

Illustration of the app's output, not a real letter. Educational triage to help you decide what to do next — not legal, tax or financial advice.

  • Not a black box

    Instead of a bare number you see the reasoning: which red flags are present, which required elements are missing — the validation rights a collector owes you, the parts a real DMCA notice needs — and which parts genuinely look solid.

  • It stays on your phone

    Reading and analysis happen on device. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account, and the documents are never seen by anyone else.

    An optional deeper cloud check is off by default; turned on, that one text is analysed and discarded, never stored.

  • Whatever arrived

    Tax notices, debt collection, court summons, eviction notices, cease and desist, fake invoices, recruiter messages, copyright takedowns — or anything else you paste in.

    The Russian edition is built on Russian scenarios, not translated IRS examples.
